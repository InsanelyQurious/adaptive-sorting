# ASSUMPTIONS & DOCUMENTED SUBSTITUTIONS

1. **The robot is a Universal Robots UR10e — everywhere.** An early draft of the specification named a different UR model; the user corrected this mid-build and every config, MJCF, backend/frontend label, dataset `robot_type`, test and document now says UR10e (audited 2026-09-26: no other model name remains outside this historical note). The MJCF `simulation/robots/ur10e.xml` is the official MuJoCo Menagerie UR10e — kinematics, link masses, inertias and actuator gains are byte-identical to `third_party/mujoco_menagerie/universal_robots_ur10e/ur10e.xml`; the only additions are the worktable base placement, the parallel-jaw gripper and named sites/cameras. The header text is configurable (`DEMO_TITLE` etc.).
2. **Gripper is a custom parallel-jaw gripper**, not a vendor model. Two prismatic fingers coupled by an equality constraint, 140 mm stroke, high-friction pads (μ=1.6, condim 4), 60 N clamp. Chosen over the Menagerie Robotiq 2F-85 for contact-grasp robustness under random RL exploration. Grasping is **pure contact physics** — no weld constraint, no teleportation.
3. **Object inertias are explicit and slightly inflated** (bolt axial inertia raised from ~1e-5 to 1.5e-4 kg·m²) to keep free-body rotational DOFs numerically stable under pinches. Bolt hex head is a box; shaft a capsule (analytic collisions).
4. **Compute is CPU-only** (AMD iGPU, no CUDA). The dashboard shows `DEVICE: CPU`. Training budgets are sized for CPU.
5. **Control is end-effector Cartesian delta with damped-least-squares IK** (roll/pitch locked so the gripper points down, yaw free). Policy outputs 7 values (dX dY dZ dRx dRy dRz gripper); dRx/dRy have zero scale by config (`control.max_delta_rot`) — they are real network outputs but do not move the arm. This is stated on the dashboard and README.
6. **`observation.state` includes object and bin poses from the simulator** (privileged state, equivalent to a perception module output) alongside proprioception; the overhead RGB image is also a real policy input. Pure-pixel policies were judged unlikely to learn pick-and-place on CPU in the available time.
7. **LeRobot integration boundary:** LeRobot 0.6.1 is imitation-learning centric; its training loop does not run RL. The RL loop is stable-baselines3 PPO. LeRobot is used for: observation/action naming (`observation.images.overhead`, `observation.state`, `action`), the `LeRobotDataset` writer for recorded rollouts, and a policy adapter exposing `select_action(batch)`.
8. **Rendering**: Python MuJoCo renders frames (EGL) → JPEG over WebSocket. No physics in the browser.
9. **Initial-state (reverse) curriculum for training only.** 70 % of *training* resets script the gripper (through the same IK controller and physics, no teleporting) to an intermediate state — pre-grasp hover (15 %), straddling the part with the gripper open (20 %), grasped+lifted (20 %), held above the correct bin (15 %) — so later stages get learning signal early; 30 % start from home. Evaluation and inference always reset from the home pose; reported success rates are therefore not inflated by the curriculum. Configurable in `configs/environment.yaml → task.curriculum` (set to 0 to disable).
10. **Frontend port is 3001, not 3000.** Port 3000 on this host is occupied by an unrelated application. The built dashboard is also served by the backend at `http://localhost:8000/`.
11. **Reward was re-tuned twice during the session** (documented in PROGRESS.md): run1's plain distance shaping produced a "hover at 10 cm at table height" optimum; run2 added a pre-grasp waypoint target and a push penalty; run3 added the curriculum. Archived runs remain loadable from `checkpoints/archive_run*/` (pass an absolute path to `/inference/load`).
12. **Evaluation of a training checkpoint happens on the live simulation** (Evaluation tab) and inside the trainer (every 50k steps, 10 episodes, clean env). Both are real rollouts; the live one takes over the viewport while it runs.
13. **Binary gripper command.** The 7th action is thresholded at 0 (close / open) by default (`action.gripper_mode`). A continuous aperture command made grasps of the 20 mm bolt practically undiscoverable by exploration (pads only meet at a command > +0.7 held for consecutive steps). The value the network outputs is still continuous and shown live; only its interpretation is binary.
14. **Training state at hand-over (2026-09-26 01:50, run8 ≈ 1.17M steps of 3M):** the policy approaches, grasps (≈45 % of clean evaluation episodes) and places a part in the correct bin (≈15 %), but does not yet complete the full two-part sort from the home pose (0 % in the last 20-episode evaluation). Training continues as a detached process; newer checkpoints appear in the dashboard automatically. All shown numbers come from real rollouts.
15. **Reward re-tuned a third time after the placement diagnosis (2026-09-26, see DEBUGGING.md).** The lift/approach potentials were being reset instead of charged back when the grasp was lost, so a grasp→lift→drop→re-grasp loop earned net reward, and 15 % over-bin curriculum starts taught "lifted ⇒ open". Potentials are now pure functions of simulator state, active during exactly one phase per target, and charged back on regress; the per-step alignment term was folded into the approach potential; the release penalty fires only on a commanded open away from the correct bin; curriculum shares moved from over-bin (10 %) to grasped (25 %) starts. Checkpoints trained on the old reward stay loadable (`checkpoints/archive_*`) but their reported eval rewards are not comparable with new ones.
16. **Imitation bootstrap = BC pretraining of the PPO network itself + interleaved BC auxiliary steps.** Because the demonstration policy and the RL policy share one architecture (SB3 `MultiInputPolicy`: NatureCNN on `observation.images.overhead` + MLP on `observation.state`), behaviour cloning trains the PPO policy's own parameters (MSE on the Gaussian mean, `log_std` frozen) and its value head (regressed on the demos' discounted returns in VecNormalize's normalised scale, whose statistics are seeded from the demos) — a direct weight transfer, no second network. To stop PPO's first noisy updates from erasing the BC solution, after every PPO update the trainer also runs `bc_aux_steps_per_iter` supervised minibatch steps on the demo buffer with a learning-rate scale that decays linearly to 0 over `bc_aux_timesteps` (logged as `bc/aux_*` in metrics.jsonl). This is *not* a BC loss added inside PPO's clipped objective (that would require forking SB3's `train()`); the interleaved form is equivalent in intent and version-robust. Configuration: `configs/training.yaml → bootstrap`. **Measured outcome (PROGRESS.md Phase 22):** at 300 k PPO steps the bootstrap beats training from scratch (clean-eval grasp 23 % vs 7 %) but the BC initialisation alone evaluates better than the PPO-fine-tuned result (grasp 90 %, placement 13 %, both parts 7 %) — PPO fine-tuning currently erodes the demonstrated behaviour; a constant BC term / lower lr / smaller noise variant did not fix that.
17. **Scripted demonstrations are pipeline validators, not human data and not policy behaviour.** `training/demos/scripted_controller.py` is a hand-coded state machine that reads ground-truth object/bin poses from the simulator and emits actions in the policy's action space through the normal `env.step()`. Its episodes are tagged `episode_type: scripted_demo` (sidecar `demo.json`, one LeRobotDataset directory per episode under `datasets/demos/`) and are never counted in any success-rate metric shown for the policy. Human teleop episodes are tagged `teleop_demo`. LeRobotDataset v3 has no free-form string feature besides `task`, hence the sidecar.
18. **Teleoperation uses the policy's action space and limits, at the policy's control rate.** Keys/mouse are composed in the browser into (dX dY dZ dRx dRy dRz gripper) ∈ [−1, 1]^7 and streamed over `/ws/teleop` at 20 Hz; the sim thread applies the latest command once per 100 ms control step through the same IK, joint-limit, velocity-limit and workspace clamps as the policy, and stops motion 0.5 s after the last message (dead-man). dRx/dRy are accepted but have zero effect because `control.max_delta_rot` locks roll/pitch (same as for the policy). Teleop is refused while training or inference/evaluation own the arm.
19. **Overhead camera unit is visual-only and sits entirely above the optical centre.** `overhead_camera_unit` (housing, lens barrel/ring/glass, LED, label, bracket) has `contype="0" conaffinity="0"`, its lowest face is at z = 2.001 m while the `<camera name="overhead">` is at z = 2.000 m looking down, so it cannot appear in the policy's image (verified pixel-identical before/after). The field-of-view overlay is geom group 5, rendered only when the viewport render asks for it; the learning camera renders with group 5 forced off. The 3rd-person `workcell` viewport camera was moved back/up (fovy 50→55°) so the unit is inside the frame — that camera is display-only.
20. **Checkpoint names are step counts, so a fresh run archives the previous run's checkpoints first.** `checkpoints/checkpoint_<steps>/` (Section 17 naming) would be silently overwritten by a new run reaching the same step count; `training/train.py` therefore moves everything in the checkpoint directory to `checkpoints/archive_<previous run>_<timestamp>/` when it starts a fresh (non-resume, non-smoke) run. Resumed runs keep appending. Archived checkpoints are not listed in the dashboard but load by absolute path (`checkpoints/ARCHIVES.md`).
21. **PPO fine-tuning after BC is anchored, trust-region-limited and selected by evaluation (Incremental Update 2).** (a) `PPOWithBC` adds `bc_coef(t)·MSE(policy mean, demo action)` on a fresh demo minibatch to every PPO minibatch loss (SB3 2.9 `PPO.train()` reproduced with that one extra term); the coefficient decays linearly 1.0 → 0.25 over 1 M steps and never reaches 0. This replaces the interleaved aux steps of #16 (kept, off). (b) `target_kl: 0.02` early-stops an update's epochs (KL trust region on top of the PPO clip); the learning rate starts at 0.1× and is ramped ×2 after every periodic evaluation whose score (success + 0.5·placement + 0.25·grasp) has not regressed by more than 0.05, halved otherwise — the composite is used because pure success is ≈ 0 early and would gate on noise. (c) When bootstrapping, VecNormalize statistics are seeded from the demos and frozen, and the initial-state curriculum probabilities are multiplied by 0.3 so ~79 % of training episodes start from the home pose like the evaluation protocol. (d) The trainer evaluates (20 clean episodes) *before* saving the checkpoint at that step and also evaluates/saves the BC initialisation, so every eligible checkpoint's metadata is its own evaluation; `best` (highest eval success, ties → placement rate → eval reward, only checkpoints evaluated at their own step) is what `Load best`, `Run Inference` and backend start-up pick; `latest` remains available. Reward shaping was audited (DEBUGGING.md §8) and found not to be the cause, so it was left unchanged.
22. **Physics timestep is 1 ms (was 2 ms) and every free part carries light viscous damping** (`physics.object_damping`: 0.5 N·s/m linear, 0.005 N·m·s angular). Both were added after the bracket occasionally diverged (|qvel| > 1e3 in one sub-step) when pinched between a finger pad and an 8 mm bin wall at release - 4 of 60 clean policy episodes at 2 ms, 0 of 120 afterwards (DEBUGGING.md part 3). The damping is an "air drag"-like approximation (~14 % of gravity at 1 m/s); the trained checkpoint's success on clean scenes was unchanged-to-better under it (57 % → 60-70 % on 60-episode batches). Physics cost per control step doubled.
23. **Extra / rejection parts use soft, priority-1 contacts** (solref 0.03 s, solimp 0.9-0.95) so a task part dragged into them yields instead of exploding; task objects and their variants keep the default stiff contacts so grasping is unchanged. A variant used as an *extra* part (e.g. a second bracket type on the table) keeps stiff contacts - rare and documented.
24. **"Defaults" and every preset scenario mean the clean table**: default bracket + default bolt + two bins, nothing else. Clutter is a deliberate, separately named scenario (`clutter_showcase`, `cluttered_u_bracket`) and has its own rows in the generalization scorecard. The current checkpoint was never trained with clutter (`randomization.clutter.prob` is 0); its clutter numbers are reported as measured, not as a target.
